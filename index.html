Moving to Berlin from the US, with kids

The guide I wish I'd had when I moved my family from California to Berlin.

Written from one family's move Route California → Berlin Arrived June 2026 Children 2
Before you rely on this

German administrative rules change, and appointment systems change faster. Verify anything time-sensitive against service.berlin.de. This is one family's route through the system, not legal advice — and where money or residence status is involved, the right answer is a professional, not a guide.

How to read this

Almost nothing in Berlin happens in parallel. Each step unlocks the next one, and everything hangs off a single first step: the Anmeldung, your address registration. Until it's done you are administratively invisible: no tax ID, no bank account, no childcare voucher, no school place, no child benefit.

The second thing to understand is that the constraint is almost never the process. Most of these steps take fifteen minutes at a counter. Getting to that counter takes weeks. Plan around booking lead times, not processing times.

Before you leave the US

The phase that matters most

Several of these become dramatically harder — or impossible — once you're on the wrong continent.

Documents to apostille before you go

  • Children's birth certificates
  • Marriage certificate and/or divorce decree
  • Any custody or parental-authority orders

An apostille is a state-level authentication — in California, through the Secretary of State. German offices will ask for these repeatedly: Kita-Gutschein, school enrollment, Kindergeld, and any custody question. Retrieving them from abroad afterwards costs weeks and money.

Certified translations

Budget for these. German authorities generally want a court-sworn translator (beeidigter Übersetzer). This is often easier to arrange in Germany after arrival, and sometimes required.

Vaccination records

Proof of measles immunity is required for entry into any Kita or school. US immunization records use a different schedule and format — get a complete printed record from your pediatrician with dates and vaccine names, and plan to have a German pediatrician transfer it into an Impfpass.

Driver's licence

Get a certified driving record from your DMV before you leave. Whether you can simply exchange your licence or must retake tests depends on which state issued it — states hold individual reciprocity agreements with Germany, and California is not a full-reciprocity state.

Things to keep, not close

A US bank account and a US phone number. Enormous amounts of US financial 2FA are SMS-based and will not reach a German number. Closing these early is the most common self-inflicted wound of the whole move.

Your US brokerage — but don't move funds without advice first. US-domiciled ETFs and mutual funds interact badly with the German Investmentsteuergesetz, and EU-based brokers generally cannot sell them to you. This is a Steuerberater question, not a blog question.

School applications

International and bilingual schools run admissions cycles that close months ahead, entirely independently of the state Schulamt process. If a specific school matters to you, this belongs here in Phase 0 — not after arrival.

01

Days 1–14

The Anmeldung, and only the Anmeldung

Legally you must register within 14 days of moving in. 14 days Practically, Bürgeramt appointment slots are scarce. Book the moment you have a move-in date — you can book before you arrive, and you can book at any Bürgeramt in the city, not only your district's.

What actually blocks you

The Wohnungsgeberbestätigung — a signed confirmation from your landlord that you have moved in. Your rental contract is not a substitute. Your landlord is legally obliged to provide it within two weeks of move-in, so ask at signing rather than after.

Register the children at the same appointment

Book a slot per person. If you are co-parenting or divorced you will also be declaring which address is each child's Hauptwohnung. This is not a formality — it determines school catchment, Kindergeld eligibility and later tax treatment. Decide before you're standing at the counter.

What you needed to bring to register the children specifically — birth certificates, both parents' consent, anything else?

What you walk out with

The Meldebescheinigung. Make several copies immediately. You will be asked for it constantly for the next three months.

Coverage is mandatory from day one of residence, and gaps can be charged retroactively. If you aren't employed yet you'll need to sort out how you're insured in the interim — one of the genuinely thorny US→Germany transition points, and worth getting right early.

03

Days 30–90

Children's places

This is where two children diverge into completely different processes — and where the naming gets confusing.

Younger child — Kita

  1. Apply for the Kita-Gutschein at the Jugendamt of your district. Not the Kita's district. Yours.
  2. The voucher specifies an hours band. This matters more than it sounds: Kitas favour children with larger vouchers because they're funded better, so the band you're granted affects how easy it is to find a place at all.
  3. The voucher and the place are two separate hunts. Having a voucher doesn't get you a spot; having a spot offer doesn't fund it. Run both searches at once.
What you were actually granted, and what the Jugendamt asked for. Did you need proof of employment or job-seeking status?

Older child — Grundschule and after-school care

  1. Register with the Schulamt of your district. State primary school is free and catchment-based.
  2. Children without sufficient German are typically routed through a Willkommensklasse for around a year before joining a regular class. Worth understanding in advance — it's a significant decision point for a US family weighing a bilingual or international school instead.
You can't file this yet · needs the school place first

After-school care is the Hortgutschein / eFöB (ergänzende Förderung und Betreuung) — a different voucher from the Kita-Gutschein, applied for through the school rather than the Jugendamt. For grades 1–3 the core hours are free and no proof of employment is required, which genuinely matters if you're arriving without a job yet.

Months 3–6

The slow burn

Driver's licence

You may drive on a valid US licence for six months from establishing residence. 6-month limit After that you need a German one, and the Umschreibung takes months to process — so start around month two or three, not month five.

Process, cost, and what the California situation meant in practice.

Your US tax obligations do not end

US citizens file US returns from abroad, permanently, regardless of where the income is earned. Add FBAR reporting once combined foreign account balances cross the threshold. Budget for a preparer who handles both sides.

Pension and social security

The US and German systems interact through a totalization agreement, and there are German credits for child-rearing years (Kindererziehungszeiten) that Americans routinely don't know to claim. A Kontenklärung with the Deutsche Rentenversicherung is the starting point.
