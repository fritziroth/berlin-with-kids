Moving to Berlin from the US, with kids

The guide I wish I'd had when I moved my family from California to Berlin.

Written from one family's move Route California → Berlin Arrived June 2026 Children 2
Before you rely on this

German administrative rules change, and appointment systems change faster. Verify anything time-sensitive against service.berlin.de. This is one family's route through the system, not legal advice — and where money or residence status is involved, the right answer is a professional, not a guide.

How to read this

Not many things in Berlin happen in parallel. Each step unlocks the next one, and everything hangs off a single first step: the Anmeldung, your address registration. Until it's done you are administratively invisible: no tax ID, no childcare voucher, no school spot, no child benefit (Kindergeld).

The second thing to understand is that the constraint is almost never the process. Most of these steps take fifteen minutes at a counter, or printing, signing and mailing in a form. But getting to that counter can take a couple weeks.

00

Before you leave the US

The phase that matters most

Several of these become dramatically harder — or impossible — once you're on the wrong continent.

Documents to apostille before you go

  • Children's or parents' US birth certificates. If the family member also has a German birth certificate, this step is not needed - simply use the German certificate in Germany
  • Marriage certificate and/or divorce decree
  • Any custody or parental-authority orders

An apostille is a state-level authentication — in California, through the Secretary of State. German offices might ask for these. Retrieving them from abroad afterwards costs weeks and money.

Certified translations

Budget for these. German authorities might want a court-sworn translator (beeidigter Übersetzer). This is often easier to arrange in Germany after arrival, and sometimes required.

Vaccination records

Proof of measles immunity is required for entry into any Kita (preschool) or school. US immunization records use a different schedule and format — get a complete printed record from your pediatrician with dates and vaccine names, and plan to have a German pediatrician transfer it into an Impfpass.

Driver's license

Bring your US driver's license. Whether you'll face exams for a German driver's license depends on which US state issued your license. That's covered in the last section.

Things to keep, not close

A US bank account and a US phone number. Enormous amounts of US financial 2FA are SMS-based and will not reach a German number. Closing these early is the most common self-inflicted wound of the whole move.

Your US brokerage or retirement account if you have one — don't move funds without advice first. US-domiciled ETFs and mutual funds interact badly with the German Investmentsteuergesetz, and EU-based brokers generally cannot sell them to US citizens. This is a Steuerberater (CPA) question.

School applications

International and bilingual schools run admissions cycles that close months ahead of the start of the school year, sometimes entirely independently of the state Schulamt (school agency) process. If a specific school matters to you that's not your neighborhood (catchment) school (Einzugsschule), this belongs here in Phase 0 — not after arrival.

Preschool (Kita) applications

Berlin preschools (Kita) follow the typical school calendar and admit most kids in the late summer or fall. If you plan to move in the summer and get your kid started in Kita, you need to find a Kita months ahead of time (January/February) to secure a spot. If you're moving mid-school year, it still helps to contact Kitas as early as possible ahead of time to find a spot at a Kita you like. The easiest way to do this is to contact the Kitas directly via email or via their websites.

Preschool voucher (Kita-Gutschein)

Berlin preschools are free. But in order to secure a spot you need a Kita voucher (Kita-Gutschein) from the local district Jugendamt. Surprisingly, Berlin district Jugendämter will issue a temporary (befristeter) Kita-Gutschein even if you're not registered yet. You can organize this ahead of time by emailing the district Jugendamt.

01

Days 1–14

The Anmeldung

Legally you must register within 14 days of moving in. 14 days Practically, Bürgeramt appointment slots are scarce but not as scarce as they used to be. Book when you know your move-in date — you can book before you arrive (often most easily done by emailing the local Bürgeramt), and you can book at any Bürgeramt in the city, not only your district's.

What actually blocks you

The Wohnungsgeberbestätigung — a signed confirmation from your landlord that you have moved in. Your rental contract is not a substitute. Your landlord is legally obliged to provide it within two weeks of move-in, so ask when you know your move-in date.

Register the children (and if possible, the whole family) at the same appointment

Book a slot for the whole family. If you are co-parenting or divorced you will also be declaring which address is each child's Hauptwohnung. This is not a formality — it determines school catchment, Kindergeld (child benefit) eligibility and later tax treatment. Decide before you're standing at the counter.

Bring birth certificates, valid passports (German and any other nationalities), expired German passports or Personalausweise if you're still in possession of them, marriage certificate or divorce decree if your marriage or divorce has never been registered in Germany, Wohungsgeberbestätigung

What you walk out with

The Meldebescheinigung! You will be asked for it constantly for the next few months.

Kindergeld is backdated a maximum of six months from application. 6-month clock Every month you delay past that is money you simply don't get. Watch for the tax IDs arriving in the mail — that's the milestone that unblocks it.

Health insurance is more urgent than it looks

Coverage is mandatory from day one of residence (they might also ask for a copy of your plane ticket as evidence of when you arrived in addition to your Meldebescheinigung), and gaps will be charged retroactively. If you aren't employed yet you'll need to sort out how you're insured in the interim — one of the genuinely thorny US→Germany transition points.

If you were insured in Germany before, contact your previous insurance provider and ask for Familienversicherung.
03

Days 30–90

Children's places

This is where kids diverge into completely different processes depending on their age — and where the naming gets confusing.

Preschool-aged child — Kita (Kindergarten)

  1. Apply for the Kita-Gutschein at the Jugendamt of your district. Not the Kita's district. Yours.
  2. The voucher specifies an hours band. This matters more than it sounds: Kitas favour children with larger vouchers because they're funded better, so the band you're granted affects how easy it is to find a place. Kita spots are generally not as scarce as a few years ago, though.
  3. The voucher and the place are two separate hunts. Having a voucher doesn't get you a spot; having a spot offer doesn't fund it. Run both searches at once.
You are eligible for part-time Kita (5-7 hours a day) even if you're not working. In other words, in order to get a Teilzeit-Kita-Gutschein, you do not need to submit any proof of employment. If you need more than 5-7 hours of care, you need to submit evidence as to why you need it (e.g. employment proof). You can also up the coverage at any time later should you need more hours later.

School-aged child — Grundschule (elementary school) or Oberschule (middle school/high school) and after-school care

  1. Register with the Schulamt of your district. Public school is free and catchment-based.
  2. In practice, this is done by going to the school in-person to register. The school will forward the registration to the Schulamt. If possible, email your district Schulamt ahead of time to let them know you will be relocating there.
  3. Children without sufficient German are typically routed through a Willkommensklasse for around a year before joining a regular class. Worth understanding in advance — it's a significant decision point for a US family weighing a bilingual or international school instead.
  4. If your child got a spot at a non-neighborhood school (e.g. private school, international school), you should follow their enrollment guidance. Depending on the school, you may or may not need to submit a Wechselwunschantrag (application for change of school) at your neighborhood school.
After-school care: You can't file this yet · needs the school place first

For after-school care you need the Hortgutschein — a different voucher from the Kita-Gutschein, applied for through the school (if your kid is going to their neighborhood school) or the Jugendamt.

For grades 1–3 the core Hort (after-school) hours are free and no proof of employment is required, which genuinely matters if you're arriving without a job yet. For grades 4 and up, there is a small income-based fee for after-school care and you will need to submit proof of income as part if your application for Hortgutschein so the Amt can determine what you need to pay.

If you're going the international or bilingual route

John-F-Kennedy School, Quentin-Blake, Berlin International School and similar run outside the public neighborhood school enrollment processes.

For example, John-F-Kennedy School has an extended application deadline of end of March for the following school year for families who still live abroad at the time of application. Other bilingual public schools save a few spots per class for families relocating shortly before the start of the school year, at least for 1st grade.
04

Months 3–6

The slow burn

Driver's license

You may drive on your US license for six months from the day you establish residence. 6-month limit After that it stops being valid — and driving on an expired foreign license isn't a fine, it's a criminal offence that also voids your insurance.

Which state issued your license decides almost everything. Germany lists 37 states plus DC and Puerto Rico in Anlage 11 of the Fahrerlaubnis-Verordnung as qualifying for simplified exchange. If yours is on that list you may walk out with a German license having sat no exams at all. California is not on it — which means both the theory and the practical exam.

The one real mercy: no mandatory driver training. You don't have to complete the lesson hours a German learner does, so you're looking at roughly €400–800 rather than the €3,500+ of a full Fahrschule course. You do need a Fahrschule to register you for the exams, and you name them on the application, so choose one before you apply. The theory test is available in English — confirm that when you book.

What to bring

  • Passport and Meldebescheinigung
  • Your original US licence — it's taken in and returned to the issuing state
  • Biometric photo
  • Certified translation of the licence, from the ADAC or a sworn translator
  • Sehtest from an optician, less than two years old — about €6–10
  • First-aid course (Lebensrettende Sofortmaßnahmen) — around nine hours, €40–50, from the DRK, ASB or Johanniter

Berlin takes two to four months to process these where other German cities manage it in weeks, and exam slots are the real bottleneck. Start at month one or two, not month five.

If you held a German license before

You may not need any of the above. The Fahrerlaubnis — the legal entitlement to drive — is a separate thing from the Führerschein, the plastic card. If you never surrendered the entitlement, you're doing a simple exchange into an EU card license rather than a conversion. If the original license was issued outside Berlin, you'll need a Karteikartenabschrift from that Bundesland's authority first.

Your US tax obligations do not end

US citizens have to file US returns from abroad, permanently, regardless of where the income is earned and where they live. Add FBAR reporting once combined foreign account balances cross the threshold. Budget for a preparer who handles both sides.

Pension and social security

The US and German systems interact through a totalization agreement, and there are German credits for child-rearing years (Kindererziehungszeiten) that Americans routinely don't know to claim. A Kontenklärung with the Deutsche Rentenversicherung is the starting point.
